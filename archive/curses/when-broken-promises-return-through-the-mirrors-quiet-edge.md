## Opening Reflection

Some truths return like a cold draft through an old doorway, reminding you of every vow they claimed was unbreakable. You feel the sting of repetition, the ache of witnessing a pattern they once promised to bury. This reflection steadies you before the mirror is raised: not to punish, but to let the shape of their choices reveal itself without disguise. Here, intention is a steady flame, catching no lies, warming no harm. You stand in quiet authority, allowing what is theirs to surface cleanly and return untouched.

## Invocation

Let their repeated pattern rise clear and return to its rightful source.

## Method

Set a small mirror on a stable surface, angled so it does not reflect you but offers a quiet field for what needs to be shown. Light a single candle beside it, letting the flame cast a soft edge around the glass. Speak their name once, not with venom, but with the steadiness of someone refusing to be rewritten. Recall the behavior they swore they would never repeat, allowing the memory to settle like dust on the mirror’s surface. Do not linger in hurt; observe the pattern as something traveling back to its owner. With slow breath, trace a fingertip along the mirror’s frame, inviting accountability to take its rightful shape. Say aloud that the cycle is no longer yours to carry. Imagine their repeated choice gathering, tightening, and returning along the path it came, carrying only truth—not harm, not bitterness, just clarity. When you feel the moment close, snuff the candle, turn the mirror face-down, and let the energy complete its return without further attention.

## Closure & Aftercare

After the mirror is lowered, let your shoulders drop and notice the soft looseness in your breath. You are not asking the universe to punish them; you are allowing the truth to find its proper home. Give yourself a few moments to ground, maybe with warm tea or quiet darkness. The cycle does not need your vigilance anymore. What is theirs will follow them, and what is yours can finally rest.

## Safety Notes

Keep reflections gentle; do not use the mirror on open wounds or anger.
