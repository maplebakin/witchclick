## Opening Reflection

Distorted memories can linger like smoke trapped in a closed room, reshaping the edges of what you once knew as solid. This reflection holds space for the hurt of remembering something that no longer feels true, or perhaps never was. You are not here to erase the past, only to return the warped pieces that were never yours to carry. In this rite, smoke becomes a gentle guide, revealing what has been bent by someone else’s telling. Let the truth of your own recollection rise, steady and unclouded.

## Invocation

Let warped memories release their haze and return to rightful origin.

## Method

Sit with a dim light nearby, allowing its glow to soften the boundaries of the room. Place a bowl or cup before you, something that holds weight without demanding attention. Imagine a thin column of smoke rising from its center, carrying the distorted memory that has settled into your body like grit. Do not force details; let whatever comes drift naturally. As you inhale, feel your chest expand with what is truly yours. As you exhale, picture the smoke curling away, lifting the warped recollection from your grasp. Speak softly about the sensation of the memory’s distortion, not the event itself. Let the smoke rise higher, carrying the bent narrative toward the place it originated. Touch the bowl’s rim with your fingertips, grounding the intention that what belongs to another may return. When the smoke in your mind loosens, release one long breath to complete the turning.

## Closure & Aftercare

Rest for a moment in the quiet that follows, letting your breath settle into something steady. Notice how the memory feels now that its distortion has lifted from your shoulders. You do not need to cling to what was twisted by another’s voice. Let the remaining clarity feel cool and honest against your ribs. When you rise, carry the truth that your recollection is allowed to be whole, and anything misshapen may drift back to its proper place.

## Safety Notes

Stay grounded; pause if emotions intensify and return when your breath feels steady.
