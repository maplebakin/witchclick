## Opening Reflection

There is a particular clarity that arrives when someone reveals more than they meant to. In an unguarded moment, their words slip, their tone shifts, or a detail surfaces that contradicts the story they’ve been curating. It can feel startling, even disorienting, to witness truth spilling out by accident. This rite is not about holding that slip against them, nor about magnifying it. It is about acknowledging the revelation without carrying the emotional debris it stirred. You allow the mirror to hold what surfaced while you reclaim your own steadiness and step back from the distortion.

## Invocation

Let the truth they revealed rest where it belongs.

## Method

Sit before a mirror in muted light, allowing the room to feel still but not tense. Place the mirror at an angle that reflects only the space around you, not your full face. Picture the accidental truth they spoke as a small shard of light—sharp, unexpected, but honest. With each measured breath, imagine that shard settling onto the surface of the mirror, no longer cutting inward. Lift your hand and hover your palm above the glass without touching it. Whisper that what they revealed belongs to its own path, not to your body. Visualize the mirror absorbing the fragment, holding it neutrally, without amplification or judgment. You are not responsible for their slip, nor for the consequences that follow it. Your task is simply to stop carrying its weight. When your breath feels smoother and your chest less taut, lower your hand and sit with the quiet that forms after understanding.

## Closure & Aftercare

Lean back slightly and let your shoulders soften. Turn the mirror face-down, allowing the energy of the moment to settle out of your field. You do not chase meaning or revisit their words. What was revealed is no longer yours to process or protect. Rest in the clarity that comes from release, trusting that the truth will travel wherever it needs to, without your involvement.

## Safety Notes

Do not imagine outcomes. Keep focus inward. Pause if agitation grows.
