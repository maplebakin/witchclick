## Opening Reflection

There comes a moment when the weight someone created finally circles back to them. You may feel the tremor of it before anyone else does—the subtle shift in the air, the quiet unraveling of stories that once held power over you. This rite is not about celebrating their struggle or wishing harm. It is about releasing your involvement in consequences that were never yours to manage. You acknowledge what’s unfolding and step out of its path, trusting that what they set in motion will continue without needing your silence, your labor, or your protection.

## Invocation

Let what they created return gently to its origin.

## Method

Place a small bowl before you, empty and clean. Sit with your breath steady and your hands relaxed at your sides. Imagine the consequences they set in motion—choices made lightly, words scattered carelessly, actions taken without regard—gathering as faint motes of dust in the air. With each slow exhale, picture those motes drifting toward the bowl, settling into a soft, shimmering layer. You do not push, pull, or direct them; you witness their natural return. Whisper that what belongs to them can find its way home without passing through your body. Place your palm over the bowl, not to seal it, but to acknowledge the boundary between you and their unfolding path. Visualize the bowl holding the energy neutrally, simply allowing it to rest where it was always meant to. When the air around you feels clearer and your breath loosens, lift your hand and sit with the stillness that follows.

## Closure & Aftercare

Gently cover the bowl with a cloth and set it aside. You do not need to watch what happens next or stay tethered to their story. The consequences belong to their choices, not your heart. Let your breath settle into its natural rhythm and feel your body reclaim space that confusion once occupied. Rest in the knowledge that you have stepped out of the way, allowing truth and consequence to meet without your interference.

## Safety Notes

Keep attention on breath. Avoid imagining harm. Pause if emotions rise sharply.
