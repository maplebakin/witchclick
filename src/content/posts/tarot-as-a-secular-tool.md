## Opening Reflection

In this guide, secular tarot means using cards as structured prompts, not as predictions or outside authority. Each image is a small museum of symbols that invites your brain to free-associate. You notice a pattern, write it down, and choose a next step. Think of it like a creative brief: the deck supplies a layout; your mind supplies the meaning.

## Why it works without belief

- Projective prompts: ambiguous images surface what is already on your mind.
- Constrained randomness: a shuffled draw reduces bias while giving you something concrete to react to.
- Externalization: cards + notes create distance, and distance creates perspective.
- Decision hygiene: a repeatable format (same spread, same questions) reduces noise so moments are easier to compare.

## Quick Reset (Low-Energy): Map-My-Moment (3 cards)

Card slots
1) Signal — what stands out right now
2) Friction — what is complicating the picture
3) Move — one small, doable next step

How to draw
1. Shuffle while naming your context (work task, conversation, messy house energy).
2. Cut once; draw three cards left to right.
3. Photograph the layout if you want to compare later.

How to read
- Scan colors, posture, and direction of figures.
- Write one plain sentence for each card: Signal, Friction, Move.

If you get flooded, use a walkaway cooldown first and return with a timer.

## Deep Ritual

Make a simple tea ritual (peppermint is great). While it steeps, open your journal or a pocket notebook.

1) Title the page with your context.
2) Pull the same three cards.
3) For each card, write one sentence plus one tiny task you can do in 10–15 minutes.
4) Optional cozy add-ons (still secular): a smooth stone as a tactile fidget, a cozy game idle in the background, or a soft playlist. These cues help your brain recognize the ritual and drop in faster next time.

## Checklist

- Name the situation in seven words or fewer.
- One sentence per card: Signal, Friction, Move.
- Translate Move into a 10–15 minute task and schedule it.
- Note one thing you learned.
- Revisit last week’s pages for 3 minutes on Sunday.

## Safety Note & Ethics

- No medical, legal, or financial advice here.
- Consent matters; read for your choices, not someone else’s secret life.
- If anxiety spikes, downshift to self-soothing and try later. Skip candles/open flame.

## Reflection Prompt

- What am I protecting right now?
- What would repair look like in one sentence?
- What is one kind sentence I can offer?
- After I act: what changed in my body?

## When to use it and when to skip it

Use it when you feel foggy, avoidant, or emotionally tangled and you need the smallest possible next step. Skip it during acute crisis or when legal/medical stakes require professional help. Ask for human support and postpone the spread.

## Quick FAQ for curious brains

Do I have to memorize meanings? No. Start with the image in front of you and your one-sentence notes.

What if I draw scary cards? Treat them as tone markers, not omens. Example: the Tower can mean “name the hard truth so you can act.”

Can I repeat the spread? Yes—give it a day unless the context truly changed.

## Keep going: related reads and gentle next step

Pick one context today and run the Map-My-Moment spread. Write three sentences, choose a 10–15 minute task, and do it before you check social media.

Next: explore a tea ritual for focus, try a game-night spread, set up a calm space, or play with worldbuilding tarot to keep the practice light.

## Conclusion

Return to this spread whenever you need a simple reset. The cards don't hold answers—they hold space for the patterns you're already tracking. Keep your notes in one place, review them on Sunday evenings, and watch how small actions compound into clarity.
